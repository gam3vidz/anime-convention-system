Delta H Con 2027 · Volunteer experience

Four ways to build the volunteer dashboard

Four complete, clickable layout directions for the volunteer-facing home screen — each driven by the exact same Delta H data so you compare layout and interaction, not content. Every card, timer, and claim button works. None of these touch the production app.

Same volunteer in every mockup: Jamie Rivera, Guest Relations, approved for Delta H Con 2027. Requirement 16 hours — 6 completed, 12 scheduled. Next up: Friday 2:00 PM Registration Desk A. Open shifts, one unread announcement, an approved crash space in Room 814.
Direction 01

Command Center

Dense, dark operations console with a persistent left rail and coverage metrics.

Best for: experienced volunteers & coordinators who want everything at a glance.

Strengths

  • Maximum information density
  • Live coverage & hour metrics
  • Feels like a real ops tool

Tradeoffs

  • Heavier for first-timers
  • Small type on phones
  • More to scan

Direction 02 Recommended

My Con Weekend

Mobile-first personal itinerary: chronological timeline, day chips, one big next-action card.

Best for: ordinary volunteers — the 90% who just need to know where to be next.

Strengths

  • Clear "what's next" focus
  • Friendly, low-friction
  • Great on a phone in a hallway

Tradeoffs

  • Less dense for power users
  • Coordinators may want more
Open My Con Weekend →
Direction 03

Shift Marketplace

Open-shift discovery first: filterable cards, coverage & urgency labels, conflict guards.

Best for: conventions that need volunteers to self-serve into understaffed shifts.

Strengths

  • Drives coverage of gaps
  • Filters & urgency signals
  • Blocks conflicting claims

Tradeoffs

  • Buries "my schedule"
  • Can feel transactional
Open Shift Marketplace →
Direction 04

Calm Check-In

Bright, high-contrast, accessibility-first: large controls, minimal load, explicit steps.

Best for: first-time volunteers and noisy, high-stress venue conditions.

Strengths

  • Largest touch targets
  • Step-by-step guidance
  • Readable at a glance

Tradeoffs

  • Lower density
  • More scrolling

Recommendation: 02 — My Con Weekend. Most volunteers open the dashboard on a phone, between tasks, to answer one question: where do I go next? The itinerary layout answers that in the first screen with a single large next-action card, keeps the clock-in and claim actions one tap away, and stays friendly without hiding the schedule. Command Center is the right power-user companion; Marketplace and Calm Check-In are strong for specific audiences, but My Con Weekend is the best default for the whole roster.

All four pages share one dataset and the same core actions (clock in/out, view shift, browse & claim open shifts, mark announcement read, switch day). Serve locally with python3 -m http.server from the repo root, then open /sketches/volunteer-dashboard/. Everything also works over file://. These are review artifacts — production lives in index.html / core.js.
